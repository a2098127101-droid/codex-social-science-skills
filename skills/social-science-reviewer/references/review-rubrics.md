# Review Rubrics

## Overall judgment labels

- Submit-ready: rare; the text has a clear question, defensible design, adequate evidence, and controlled claims.
- Major revision: promising but needs substantial redesign, reframing, or additional evidence.
- Restructure before submission: core idea may be viable, but architecture and evidence logic are not yet publishable.
- Not submission-ready: foundational gaps in question, theory, method, evidence, or ethics.
- Rejection-level risk: flaws likely to trigger desk rejection or decisive reviewer rejection.

## Fatal or near-fatal problems

- Research question is trivial, unanswerable, or too broad.
- Literature gap is fabricated by selective review.
- Method cannot answer the question.
- Causal claim lacks causal design.
- Data are insufficient for the claim.
- Core concept cannot be operationalized or interpreted.
- Model has leakage, invalid comparison, or missing baseline.
- Qualitative sample or case logic cannot support the conclusion.
- Ethics approval or consent is missing for sensitive human-subject work.
- References, data, interviews, or results are fabricated.

## Structural but repairable problems

- Theory framework is loose.
- Literature review lists authors instead of debates.
- Concept boundaries are unstable.
- Case/sample selection is underjustified.
- Robustness, negative cases, or alternative explanations are weak.
- Results section overinterprets output.
- Discussion does not return to the research question.
- Methods are not reproducible enough.
- Tables and figures do not directly support claims.

## Surface-level problems

- Academic style is uneven.
- Terms are inconsistent.
- Abstract is too promotional.
- References are incomplete or inconsistently formatted.
- Tables lack notes or clear variable labels.
- Paragraphs are too long or repetitive.
- English is literal translation from Chinese.
- Chinese prose is policy-slogan-heavy or inflated.

## Revision plan format

For each major issue, specify:

1. Where to revise
2. What to delete or rewrite
3. What evidence or literature to add
4. What claim to downgrade
5. What check or robustness analysis to run
6. What remains a limitation
